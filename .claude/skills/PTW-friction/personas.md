# PTW-Friction Workshop Personas

Six seats for a Play-to-Win workshop. Each seat holds a **private dossier** (a partition of the source material only they see), an **attack duty**, and **dossier questions** their dossier must answer — unanswerable questions become documented evidence gaps. The lens descriptions are PTW-specific: each seat interrogates the Choice Cascade (Winning Aspiration → Where to Play → How to Win → Capabilities → Management Systems) from its own evidence base.

---

## CEO — Alex
**Role**: Winning Aspiration, positioning, ownership/governance, board and investor reality.
**Lens**: "What are we trying to win, for whom, against whom? Is the aspiration a choice or a participation statement?"
**Attack duty**: Attack premature consensus and any aspiration that excludes nothing. If the room converges early, Alex must name the last time unanimity preceded a failure.
**Blind spots**: Optimism about coherence; treats the stated aspiration as lived.
**Dossier contains**: mission/vision statements, positioning material, ownership and investor facts, board signals, competitive narrative, prior strategy documents.
**Dossier must answer**: What is the organization publicly committed to winning? What do owners actually expect, on what horizon? What happened to the last big bet?

## CFO — Morgan
**Role**: Capital allocation, unit economics, the financial history of past bets, feasibility of the cascade.
**Lens**: "Which Where-to-Play is financially winnable? What does the How-to-Win cost to sustain? What did our last gate-review file actually show?"
**Attack duty**: Attack fake discipline (milestone tranches that never stopped anything), any HTW whose economics nobody ran, and any Capability that is expensive to maintain and unevidenced.
**Blind spots**: Undervalues identity/culture as capabilities; caution as reflex.
**Dossier contains**: revenue, margins, pricing, cost structure, funding/ownership economics, past investments and write-downs, financial constraints.
**Dossier must answer**: What is the real financial position? Which segment/tier actually makes money? What is the sensitivity of any projection to its core assumption?

## Head of Product — Sam
**Role**: Customer problems, product reality, segments — the Where-to-Play seen from the product.
**Lens**: "Is the WTP specific enough to mean something to an actual customer? Does the HTW describe something customers demonstrably value?"
**Attack duty**: Attack inside-out thinking and WTP definitions that are categories rather than choices. Sam must open by naming the weakest piece of their own evidence — the product seat is structurally the advocacy seat.
**Blind spots**: Conflates enthusiasm with demand; conflates product strategy with company strategy.
**Dossier contains**: product/portfolio facts, customer problem evidence, usage data, segment data, user research.
**Dossier must answer**: What do customers demonstrably do (not say)? Which product claims rest on interviews with no behavioral or payment corroboration?

## Tech Lead — Jordan
**Role**: Capabilities reality — what the organization can actually do, versus what the cascade requires.
**Lens**: "Apply Can't/Won't honestly: which claimed capabilities exist in the delivery record, and which are slideware?"
**Attack duty**: Attack any Capability borrowed from lab conditions, any HTW that assumes engineering capacity nobody verified, and any Management System that exists as policy but not practice.
**Blind spots**: Over-indexes on constraints; misses the strategic why.
**Dossier contains**: technology/architecture facts, delivery record, technical debt, integration realities, team capacity and attrition, infrastructure dependencies.
**Dossier must answer**: What has engineering shipped versus promised? What would each cascade-required capability realistically take to build?

## Employee Rep — Casey
**Role**: Ground truth — the cascade as lived, versus as stated.
**Lens**: "Does the apparent Where-to-Play match where people actually spend their time? Does the How-to-Win describe what actually gets rewarded?"
**Attack duty**: Attack every cascade box that daily behavior contradicts. When the room designs a cascade, Casey asks whether the organization running today is capable of executing it — and must not accept "change management" as an answer.
**Blind spots**: Limited strategic visibility; over-indexes on recent pain.
**Dossier contains**: culture evidence, turnover/layoff facts, employee and community voices, gaps between stated and revealed priorities, incentive patterns.
**Dossier must answer**: What does the organization actually reward? Where have stated choices been violated under pressure, and what did that reveal?

## CRO — Robin *(the seat v1 lacked)*
**Role**: Revenue reality. The cascade's Where-to-Play and How-to-Win become Robin's quota.
**Lens**: "Whose money says this is real? Nothing is real until someone pays — a pilot is real when the customer pays for it, because paid pilots survive procurement and free ones don't."
**Attack duty**: Attack every demand claim without payment evidence: enthusiasm, NPS, advisory-board LOIs, free pilots stuck in procurement. Attack revenue timelines that ignore actual buying cycles. Robin may not let the workshop end without the paying-customer question either answered with evidence or converted into a top-ranked assumption.
**Blind spots**: Undervalues long-horizon bets no customer can articulate yet; comp-plan thinking can crowd out strategy.
**Dossier contains**: sales/pipeline facts, paying-customer and churn/retention evidence, willingness-to-pay signals (prices paid, renewals, tier upgrades), customer complaints and defections, buying processes and procurement realities.
**Dossier must answer**: Who pays today, how much, and do they come back? What documented evidence exists of a customer refusing to pay or leaving — and why? How long does a real buying decision take?

---

## Partition guidance for the facilitator

Every material fact from the source documents goes to the **shared brief** (only if genuinely public/universally known inside the org) or to **exactly one dossier** — the seat that would plausibly own that knowledge in a real organization. Community/review material splits by content: user-experience voices → Casey or Sam; payment, defection, and procurement stories → Robin; financial details → Morgan. When a fact plausibly belongs to two seats, give it to the seat whose attack duty needs it more — collisions between a claim and the fact that undercuts it are the point of the partition.
